    Solution to Partners in Crime

    Answer: TANGLES
    Written by: Guan Yang Ze
    Edited by: Max Chang

    This puzzle presents us with a Pokémon battle where all Pokémon species, move names, and ability names have been hidden. The natural step is to try to identify all of these. However, complicating matters further is the exact ruleset of the battle; as indicated in the battle video and pointed to by the title of the puzzle, this is a Partners in Crime battle, a Double Battle format specific to Pokémon Showdown! that has Pokémon share the abilities and moves of their allies next to them on the field. As it turns out, even under this ruleset, the Pokémon species and their abilities can be identified uniquely. (Not all moves can be identified uniquely based on the battle shown, but this doesn't matter for the puzzle.)

    Part 1: Identifying the Pokémon

    Let's start by identifying the Pokémon involved in this replay. This can be done by observing particular interactions and effects of their moves and abilities. We'll list all known information about each Pokémon as we go along.

    A few notes before we start:

    • In Partners in Crime, the moves available to Pokémon depend on which Pokémon were on the field at the start of the turn (when the moves are selected), but which abilities are active depend on the board state when the move is used. For example, if Pokémon A is next to Pokémon B at the start of the turn, but B switches to Pokémon C, A could use one of B's moves while under the effects of C's ability.
    • Every Pokémon besides Gamer A's Pokémon F1 K3 O2 has its item revealed as Leftovers or Life Orb, so items don't play a part in determining any Pokémon identities (or other aspects) in this battle. (To be rigorous, we also never use F1 K3 O2's item as the basis for a deduction.)

    First, on Turn 14, Q3 Y7 uses a move that damages both opponents and lowers its own Special Attack by one stage. The only move fitting this criteria is Make It Rain, which is the signature move of Gholdengo.

    Also on Turn 14, the other Pokémon on Gamer A's side, H5 P3 X6, uses a move which twists the dimensions (only Trick Room has this text) before it is forced out by a non-damaging move (which must be either Roar or Whirlwind). Note that Trick Room has -7 priority while moves that force the opponent to switch out have -6 priority, so this interaction should never happen. Therefore Trick Room's priority must have been raised somehow (since no effects in the game lower priority from one bracket to the next); the only possibility is that Trick Room was used under the effects of Prankster. Gholdengo itself can't learn Trick Room, so the two Pokémon on Gamer A's side are Gholdengo and a Prankster user with Trick Room (so Klefki, Meowstic, or Whimsicott), in some order.

    Additionally, observe that H5 P3 X6 is under the effects of its partner Gholdengo's ability, Good as Gold, which should prevent the status moves Roar or Whirlwind from affecting it. Therefore the ability must have been ignored somehow; there are a few abilities that could ignore opposing abilities in this way, but almost all of them announce themselves with text when a Pokémon enters battle (such as Mold Breaker). The lone exception is Mycelium Might, which is the signature ability of Toedscruel. Therefore one of the Pokémon on B's side, C4 G2 J1 or L4 M5 S3, must be Toedscruel.

    Now we just have to determine the identity of V5 AA2. Observing the turns where it's on the field, particularly Turn 8 and Turn 9, we notice another interesting speed interaction: on Turn 8, Incineroar moves before Arcanine, whereas on Turn 9, Arcanine moves before Incineroar.     Part 2: Determining move priorities

    As directed by the cluephrase, we must determine which moves in the battle are used with positive priority. In general, this requires more movepool-checking than the previous part, since some deductions hinge on knowing that certain pairs of Pokémon don't have damaging priority moves in their movepools, or that certain classes of moves can't be used at increased priority. Once we reach a move in a turn which must have a priority of 0 or lower, we can stop looking at the other moves used, since they must also be used at non-positive priorities.

    Turn 1

    Clodsire's Tailwind is used under Gale Wings and at full HP, so it's used at +1 priority. Talonflame moves next, which implies that it must be moving at +0 priority, since it's impossible for Clodsire to outspeed Talonflame if they use moves at the same priority.

    Turn 2

    Talonflame's Taunt is the first move used. Under the circumstances, it has a priority of +0.

    Turn 3

    Arcanine moves first with Baneful Bunker, which has +4 priority. Talonflame is next to move, using a damaging pivot move, which can never be boosted to positive priority.

    Turn 4

    Clodsire moves first with a redirection move; both moves capable of redirecting moves to the user (Follow Me and Rage Powder) have +2 priority. Toedscruel moves next and uses a draining move; without Triage, all such moves have priority +0.

    Turn 5

    Gholdengo moves first with Protect, which has +4 priority. Toedscruel uses a draining move, which has +0 priority as before.

    Turn 6

    Noivern moves first and uses a draining move, which has +0 priority as before.

    Turn 7

    Noivern moves first, but Noivern and Toedscruel have no priority moves between them. The only way the move could be boosted is if it's a draining move, since Comfey has just switched in. But it's not, so Noivern's move is used at +0 priority.

    Turn 8

    Noivern moves first with the Triage-boosted draining move at +3. As discussed in the previous section, Incineroar uses a priority move here, which could be Fake Out or Extreme Speed. It's not Incineroar's first turn on the field, so it can't be Fake Out, so Incineroar uses Extreme Speed at +2. Arcanine moves next with Knock Off, which is +0 in the given circumstance.

    Turn 9

    Arcanine moves first with a damaging pivot move, which must have +0 priority in the given circumstance.

    Turn 10

    Toedscruel moves first with Prankster Haze due to the Tornadus switch-in, giving it a priority of +1. Due to the effects of Mycelium Might, it must be the last move used in its priority bracket, so there are no other priority moves used this turn.

    Turn 11

    Tornadus moves first with the move which we uniquely identified as Bleakwind Storm above. Under the given circumstances, it has a priority of +0.

    Turn 12

    Talonflame moves first with Tailwind, which is boosted by both Gale Wings and Prankster to reach a priority of +2. Tornadus moves next and uses a damaging move which is super-effective on Gholdengo, so it can't be Flying-type (and so is not boosted by either ability). None of the boosted-priority moves learnt by Talonflame and Tornadus are super-effective on Gholdengo, either, so we conclude that this move is used at +0 priority.

    Turn 13

    Tornadus uses a healing move on Comfey, which must either be Floral Healing or Pollen Puff. The former would be +4 priority due to being boosted by both Triage and Prankster, while the latter is boosted by neither and would be +0 priority. Gholdengo moves next and uses a recovery move, which is boosted to +1 priority by Prankster, so we conclude that Tornadus's move must have been the +4 Floral Healing. Finally, Comfey uses a damaging pivot move, which must have a priority of +0 given the circumstances.

    Turn 14

    Whimsicott moves first with Make It Rain which must have +0 priority.

    Turn 15

    Incineroar moves first with an attack that hits both opponents and lowers their Special Attack, which must be Snarl. It has a priority of +0 in these circumstances.

    Turn 16

    Talonflame moves first with Roost, which is boosted by Triage but not Gale Wings, since Talonflame doesn't have full HP, so its priority is +3. Incineroar moves next with Snarl, as in the previous turn, which has +0 priority again.

    Turn 17

    Incineroar moves first with Helping Hand, which has +5 priority and is further boosted to +6 by Prankster. Clodsire also uses Helping Hand, but at the base +5 priority, since there's no Prankster on B's side. Whimsicott moves next and uses a damaging move, but Whimsicott and Clodsire have no damaging priority moves in their movepool, so it must be used at +0 priority.

    Author's Notes

    It's pretty funny how Pokémon puzzles have become almost ubiquitous in puzzlehunts especially over the last few years, growing to the point of practically becoming a yearly Mystery Hunt staple (to the point where one annual Mystery Hunt prediction game I follow had to take out the question "Will there be a puzzle about Pokémon?" because the answer was always yes). This informed and also sort of "allowed" the design of this puzzle—despite the frequency of nominally Pokémon-flavored puzzles, I've always felt like these puzzles don't really have a true connection to the experience of playing Pokémon, which compelled me to write a puzzle that would resonate with (and reward!) people truly "fluent" in Pokémon mechanics. On the other hand, with how popular of a topic Pokémon has become, it also felt like a reasonable assumption that many top teams would have someone sufficiently fluent to solve this puzzle.

    Partners in Crime is by far my favorite competitive format, and I think its dynamism made it a uniquely perfect fit for this puzzle—I think having to figure out which Pokémon actually owns a given move or ability makes it feel a bit more puzzly and a bit less like a lookup bash. Practically speaking, too, using moves from partners on switch-outs and applying abilities from partners on switch-ins is highly beneficial for condensing a lot of information into a pretty short replay, which I think made the puzzle less draggy. Starting from a shortlist of interactions and deductions I wanted to use in the puzzle, I was able to orchestrate a pretty compact battle sequence to fit all of them in and get the necessary moves for extraction. Some that didn't make it into the actual puzzle were "Prankster Gale Wings Tailwind outspeeding Extreme Speed", "proccing Regenerator by switching out the same turn an ally with Regenerator switches in", and "Levitate switch-in stopping Grassy Glide from getting priority".

    As a final fun fact, while writing this puzzle I discovered that actional priority is modified so early in the turn that switching in Toedscruel applies the ability-ignoring effect of Mycelium Might to its partner, but not the end-of-bracket lowered priority effect! I thought this was a simulator bug until I spoke with a sim programmer about it, and the interaction felt so unintuitive and undocumented that I cut it from the puzzle.
