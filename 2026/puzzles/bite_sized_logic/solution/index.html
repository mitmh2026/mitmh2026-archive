    Solution to Bite-sized Logic

    Answer: WEIGHTED SHIPPING
    Written by: Cindy Jiang
    Edited by: Herman Chau
    Additional thanks to:
    • Herman Chau for writing the Roma puzzle, editing, generating the art, and helping with the postprod
    • Eric Higgins for writing the Ham Sandwich puzzle

    This puzzle consists of eight logic puzzle types that all have food-related titles.

    The rules for normal square grids can be found online. For this puzzle, the rules are generalized to work on a hexagonal grid instead. The grayed out tiles are not used in the logic puzzles.

    The solved levels are shown below.

    Choco Banana

    Shade black some tiles on the board.

    1. A group of shaded tiles must form a parallelogram of hex tiles.
    2. A group of unshaded tiles must not form a parallelogram of hex tiles.
    3. A number indicates the size of the (shaded or unshaded) group that overlaps it. A group can contain one or more numbers, or none at all.

    Coffee Milk

    Draw lines between the circles to form groups.

    1. Lines between circles must be straight, and cannot turn.
    2. Lines cannot cross each other.
    3. Each group of circles must contain exactly one gray circle, and an equal amount of white and black circles. The number of white and black circles may be 0.
    4. A black circle and a white circle cannot be connected directly.

    Ham Sandwich

    Fill some tiles of the grid with squares or circles.

    1. The given numbers indicate the number of circles which appear between the squares in the corresponding row and column. Axes with no given numbers have no constraints.
    2. Each line along an indicated axis contains two squares and N circles. The number of circles is given via N = 5 in the puzzle.

    Lollipops

    Fill some tiles of the grid with a symbol of one of four types: a circle, a horizontal line, a / line, or a \ line.

    1. Symbols must form pairs of size 1x2 or 2x1 consisting of a circle and a line extending from it. Some symbols are given.
    2. These two-tile pairs cannot be adjacent to one another.
    3. Two tiles with the same symbol cannot share a line of sight with each other, unless another symbol is between them.

    Milk Tea

    Draw lines to connect groups of three circles with a Y shape. The trunk of the Y can be tilted in either direction.

    1. Each group must be connected by a Y shaped line: a straight line with an angled line extending from somewhere in its middle. The angled line cannot extend from either endpoint. The Y may be upright, sideways, or upside down.
    2. Circles are always the endpoints of a line.
    3. On the Y shape, the two circles connected by the same line must be white. These circles may not be in orthogonally adjacent tiles.
    4. The third circle must be black. The stem from the black circle does not have to be centered along the top of the Y between the white circles.
    5. Y shapes from different groups may not overlap or share circles, and every circle has to be used.

    Norinori

    Shade some black tiles on the board.

    1. Every region contains exactly two black tiles.
    2. Each black tile must be a part of a 1 x 2 block (domino) in any direction. The domino can be part of one or two regions.
    3. No two dominoes may share an edge.

    Roma

    Draw arrows pointing in six directions in the empty tiles.

    1. The arrows in each region must all be different.
    2. Starting with any tile, following the arrows from tile to tile, this path must end in the tile with the black circle.

    Takoyaki

    Draw multiple paths through all tiles without numbers, then place circles on top of them.

    1. Every path goes through exactly three circles: One on each endpoint, and another somewhere in the middle.
    2. Paths cannot cross or branch off, and cannot form loops. Paths may turn at the center of a tile.
    3. Two circles cannot be orthogonally adjacent.
    4. Circles belonging to the same path cannot share a hexagonal row in any direction.
    5. A number indicates the number of circles in the 6 orthogonally adjacent tiles.
    6. Paths and circles cannot overlap tiles with numbers in them.

    The prices in the submission tile are determined by counting all tiles across the logic puzzles that match the pattern shown on each shell tile. Once all levels containing a specific tile are solved, the corresponding count is displayed automatically.

    Note: Circles and lines are treated as independent features; for example, a tile containing a circle with a line through it contributes to both a pattern with a circle and a pattern with a matching line.

    The clues at the bottom can be combined with the logic puzzle titles to form new food items.

    The first column of clues is paired directly with each logic puzzle title. The results of that pairing then serve as the inputs for the second column, which is chained onto those newly formed words. In this way, each puzzle title produces two food answers in sequence: one from the first column and a second derived from the first result.

    The clues are given in alphabetical ordering and need to be reordered. The final ordering is alphabetical by logic puzzle title.

    Author's Notes

    Warning: This note contains details about the next round's mechanic.

    This puzzle was originally written in the Fate's Thread Casino. After writing Easy As 1-22-333, we wanted to make the clues harder to distinguish between the two halves, so we migrated the puzzle to the Kingdom of the Puzzmon.

    After Atlas of Mosaics writing started, it became clear to the team that they were extremely tight on time and bandwidth, especially given the constraints of the round.

    So this puzzle once again migrated!

    The final extraction step was added at this point too. The original extraction used the logic puzzle titles directly. Unfortunately it would be quite difficult to find sixteen different food-themed logic puzzles whose rules can be expressible on a hexagonal surface (not for lack of trying). But we got very lucky that the answer had enumeration (8 8); it could have been much harder to adapt to.
