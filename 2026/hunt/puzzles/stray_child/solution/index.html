    Solution to Stray Child

    Answer: MACHINE
    Written by: Tim Wong
    Edited by: Dan Simon

    We are presented with a crossword grid. Upon solving a couple of clues, we realize that most entries are too long to fit into the grid. For example, clue 1A solves to "ATLANTIC", which has 8 letters, but there are only 6 blanks.

    Looking at the list of clues, we may notice clue 36D ("Country whose language is used for filling this puzzle"). Moreover, numerous clues reference Japan-related things (e.g. clue 53A, 74A, 76A, 34D, 57D). This suggests that this is a Japanese crossword puzzle.

    We wish to find a length-3 answer for clue 36D. When written in kana, "Japan" (にほん) has a length of 3, so we should use kana for filling in the grid. The answers to the clues and their translations are listed below.

    We can fill the grid by entering one kana per cell. Compound kana like しゃ take up two cells, and sokuon (っ) takes up one cell. We can fill most of the cells this way, but there are a number of junctions that cannot be filled this way. For example, entry 3A (すなあらし) and 3D (やすらい) are too long and conflict with each other.

    As clued by the clue "??? x7", there are seven such problematic spots (highlighted in red), all of which are three cells long. We will refer to these cells as "conflicting cells". We can resolve the conflict by discarding the seven entries for the conflicting cells, then allowing multiple characters to be placed in each conflicting cell. This means that the cell content only depends on the crossing entries (highlighted in blue).

    Typically, katakana is used for Japanese crossword puzzles. The grids here use hiragana as none of the entries contain katakana (except for 「ホスト」, explained later). Small characters like 「ゅ」 and 「っ」 are usually converted to their big form 「ゆ」 and 「つ」, but we kept them as-is in the grid. They do not affect the filling or later steps.

    Rather than putting multiple characters in a cell, there is actually a way to fill the cells using only a single character per cell. Besides kana, Japanese also uses kanji. All crossing entries are made up of two kanji, with the kana in each conflicting cell corresponding to one of the kanji in each entry. This allows us to fill each cell with a single kanji, which is lightly hinted at by the clue "???", as there is only one question mark per cell.

    After filling the cells using kanji, we get seven kanji phrases of length 3. If we look up any of these kanji phrases, we find that all of them are songs by MyGO!!!!!, a band from the BanG Dream! franchise. This is confirmed by the puzzle title "Stray Child", which solves to "迷子", and the band's name is a play on the word "迷子". Note that it is also possible to do everything in reverse, finding MyGO!!!!! from the title before realizing that some cells need to be filled with songs from the band.

    Besides its music, MyGO!!!!! is also known for its difficult-to-read song titles (in the form of ateji), which have unconventional readings. Their readings can be found in various online sources, such as Bang Dream wiki and pixiv Encyclopedia (Japanese).

    We still haven’t used the answers for the original clues. If we compare the readings of the song titles (which are actual words in Japanese) to the answers of the original clues, we notice that they differ only in a single kana. Moreover, all songs come from the album "Michinoku". The track number of each song appears in the clues "6 12 3 → (4)" and "4 8 11 10 → (11)", which gives us an ordering for the kana and allows us to extract two words.

    We obtain the phrase "TIME ? / ? TRANSLATION". We can form the phrases "TIME MACHINE" and "MACHINE TRANSLATION", so the answer is MACHINE.

    Author’s Notes

    Back in January 2025, I came across the album "Michinoku" which was recently released. After noticing that every song title is three characters long and unpronounceable, I knew I had to make a puzzle about the album.

    The current cluephrase is a miraculous find as it is quite constrained. The initial version was 「こたえはきかい」but that had a bunch of issues: the album title had to be used for extraction; the clue phrase was ambiguous; and only three songs contributed to the final answer. The current cluephrase is ambiguous when translating 時間 to English, but this is resolved by the final step. "Time" also seemed to be the more natural translation in testsolves.

    At the time of writing, there are eight playable bands in the Bandori rhythm game. The three bands added in later updates after the initial release are: RAISE A SUILEN (clue 44D), Morfonica (clue 26A), and MyGO!!!!! (this puzzle).

    Sorry to Bandori fans who were hoping to see more about the franchise! This puzzle ended up focusing more on the Japanese language than the franchise. I included references to music and anime in many clues, so I hope you still enjoyed the puzzle.
