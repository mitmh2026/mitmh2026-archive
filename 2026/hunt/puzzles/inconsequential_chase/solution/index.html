    Solution to Inconsequential Chase

    Answer: SUNBEARS
    Written by: Alison Chang, Marisa, Herman Chau, and Akira Baruah
    Edited by: Herman Chau

    Overview

    There are 6 trivia-based levels. Solvers must solve 4 of the levels to unlock the center extraction tile. Each tile has a center word/phrase and 6 clues on the edges; the clues must accurately describe neighboring tiles, even across level boundaries. To solve each level, all tiles must be placed in their correct space.

    Similar to how jigsaw puzzlers often begin with the edge pieces, we can start each level by looking for “edge” tiles - those that have 1+ blank side, because they are on the outermost edge of the tile set for this feeder. From there, we can identify potential candidates based on combinations of clues, and build out until we finish the whole level.

    In some cases, the cross-level clues intentionally disguise themselves: many clues in the Pokémon level refer to a neighboring Pokémon’s generation (Gen 1, Gen 2, etc), but some say Gen Alpha or Gen Z instead; these are actually references to the Year tiles across the boundary.

    Levels

    Going clockwise from the top, the levels are:

    Level: Songs

    Every tile has the name of a Beatles song. The clues in this level vary from ones that apply to many, many options (e.g., “mentions love” or “produced by George Martin”) to ones that are much more specific (e.g., inclusion of specific instruments like harmonica, piccolo trumpet, or chocalho, “written for Julian Lennon”, “also a board game”). We can use the specific clues to guide our tile placement.

    Extraction

    The extraction card may look a little familiar, but if it doesn’t, the puzzle title Inconsequential Chase provides an extra nudge to this puzzle’s theme - Trivial Pursuit.

    In fact, the completed puzzle should look similar to a completed Trivial Pursuit pie piece with all 6 wedges collected.

    Note: brown was the original color for Arts & Literature, but this author only owns the newer version where it is purple.

    Each clue refers to a specific tile and then points out its neighbor (for example, Utah is left of Cyprus):

    The meta shell has 6 empty ovals, because the clues are given in Trivial Pursuit card order, which allows you to securely map each one to a color. Rearranging these 6 words by the provided sequence of colors under the card (pink, yellow, green, brown, orange, blue) gives you the clue phrase DISTANCE FIVE AWAY FROM CENTER HEXAGON.

    This points to the hexagons in each level which are exactly a straight distance 5 tiles away from the middle ! tile. These can be matched to the provided clues to start pointing towards specific references.

    If we then consider the two sets of 3 items and look for commonalities, we can find answers to the questions:

    1. What thing ties together Lunala counterpart, 1917 in Portugal, and Yellow Submarine second verse? SUN

      • Solgaleo is associated with the sun (mascot of Pokémon Sun, known as “the beast that devours the sun”)
      • Portugal in 1917 had multiple historic events, including Miracle of the Sun in Fátima
      • Yellow Submarine’s second verse starts with “So we sailed on to the sun”

    2. What things do Leonardo Dicaprio 2015 film, Broncos no. 6 (2006), and Alaska flag parent cluster share? BEARS

      • The Revenant prominently features a bear attack on Leonardo DiCaprio’s character
      • Jay Cutler was a member of the Chicago Bears for most of his career
      • Alaska’s flag depicts the Big Dipper, which is an asterism within the larger constellation Ursa Major (the Great Bear)

    This gives us the final answer, SUN BEARS.

    Author's Notes

    Ali

    As a jigsaw-lover, I have always enjoyed puzzles where solvers have to put together square-shaped pieces using the clues on each edge. When I heard about this crazy tile round, I immediately wanted to do something like that… but with hexagons. (Had I realized the sheer number of clues I would then have to write for this, I might’ve stopped myself there).

    After hearing my trivia pitch, Herman suggested that we pull inspiration from Trivial Pursuit, which shaped our selection of the 6 trivia topics (as well as the tile level layout). Similar to real trivia games (which I honestly usually avoid in real life), each level is definitely easier when you have existing knowledge on the subject, but we tried to test with unfamiliar folks too to make sure the levels were still solvable.

    I was excited to realize that SUN and BEARS are common enough topics that we could find a relevant entry (or two, or three) in each of our categories; Herman also had convinced me at this point that it would be difficult to draw sunbears visually in hexagonal art. We did however want to make more of the individual tiles feel useful in the final steps, which led us to 1) reference tiles via their neighbors rather than directly, and 2) we actually rewrote the extraction layer close to hunt to incorporate the DISTANCE FIVE cluephrase.

    The Pokémon level was inspired in part by a real conversation where I was preparing to do a Pokémon Go raid with a friend and asked her what we were battling; she said “I don’t know, it’s yellow and it has stripes.” (The same friend also frequently complained about how all the shinies she caught were green.)

    During the writing of these levels:

    • Akira and I realized we disagreed on the animal type of many Pokémon
    • We also went down a rabbit hole (groundhog hole?) trying to understand how exactly humans even know whether Phil saw his shadow or not (is he talking?? He’s just a groundhog!)
    • I discovered too late that I don’t really care for Beatles songs

    During testsolves:

    • Multiple people were shook to learn about the uno, dos, tres in Articuno, Zapdos, and Moltres’s names
    • At one point, solvers temporarily labeled Absol as the “new Pope”
    • Many people fell into the electric mousetrap, as we intended, and also got a good groan/laugh out of “quadruped?” for Tandemaus, also as intended 😈

    Herman

    I love flags so I was excited to get to contribute a flag level to this puzzle. Hopefully you got to learn at least one fun fact while solving it!

    Marisa

    Growing up abroad, People and ESPN were two of my primary windows into American culture. I was excited to finally put years of “studying” to good use on a puzzle!
