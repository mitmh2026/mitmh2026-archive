    Solution to Setting Boundaries

    Answer: ISOLATE
    Written by: Kuan-Yu Lin
    Edited by: Dan Simon

    First, solve the pairs of puzzles. Here are their solutions. (Yellow and blue regions are of the first and second puzzle types, respectively.)


    Water Walk / Double Choco

    Sashigane / Snake Pit

    Look-Air / FiveCells

    Heyawake / Aqre

    Balance Loop / Shikaku

    Ripple Effect / Islands

    While solving, we might notice that each puzzle type contains exactly one clue that's unknown. By filling out the unknown numbers, we get the following table:


    Puzzle typesQuestion marksIndexing result
    Water Walk / Double Choco4 / 1ED
    Sashigane / Snake Pit6 / 5GE
    Look-Air / FiveCells2 / 3OV
    Heyawake / Aqre2 / 3ER
    Balance Loop / Shikaku3 / 5LA
    Ripple Effect / Islands4 / 2PS

    Indexing into the puzzles yields the clue phrase "EDGE OVERLAPS". Following the phrase, we overlap the boundaries between the puzzle types, ditching edges that only appeared in one puzzle. The overlapped edges would then show the final answer: ISOLATE

    Appendix: Step-by-step logic of the grids

    In the following text, RxCy refers to "Row x, Column y", where R1C1 is the top-left cell. Across the grids, there is one important fact to keep in mind: There can only be at most two different puzzle types on the edges. For example, in the following grid, the edge cells between the two yellow cells must all be yellow. Otherwise, a bottom left blue cell cannot reach other blue cells without breaking the connection between yellow cells.

    From here on out, we will call this fact the "Yin-Yang rule".

    Water Walk / Double Choco

    First of all, an important observation: Each number must belong to a white region of that size. Using this rule, we can get the following grid:

    The bottom left and bottom right numbers must be in Double Choco since there's no way for the path to escape otherwise. The 5 connects to the gray region to its left. The 3, blocked by the V-shaped 5 region, must also be in Double Choco, connecting to the gray, straight region to its left. Using similar logic, we can get more Double Choco regions on the bottom.

    Now, the central white regions have no matching black regions, making them Water Walk regions. By making sure the ends escape, we get some partial paths. Notice that the 2 can’t connect with the 3 since it would block the path of 1.

    The 3 and 2 at the bottom right are in Double Choco. To avoid isolating gray regions in Double Choco, the gray region of that 3 must be vertical. To ensure the path loops, the 2 at R1C7 must be part of the path, which makes the 2 next to it part of the path too. The top right 2 is in Double Choco using the same logic we used at bottom left. We can then use the Yin-Yang rule to assign the rightmost column to Double Choco.

    The 3 at R3C10 must be L-shaped, connecting to the bottom dark region. The loop must now pass through the 3 at R5C8. If the loop tries to pass through the ? at top right, the 2 next to it would have no dark cells to pair up. Therefore R2C8 and R3C8 directly connect, completing the right side of the loop. The 2 at the top right can’t be horizontal since that would leave a stray Double Choco cell. The ? next to it must be a Double Choco clue of 1. The top left ? can't be in Double Choco since it would result in blocked paths. Thus this ? and the 2 next to it belong to Water Walk, completing the path.

    Sashigane / Snake Pit

    We can easily see that both the bottom left 6 and the bottom right 7 can't be Sashigane clues, making them part of Snake Pit. We then put our focus on the 5 pair at R5C1. If the top clue is a Snake Pit clue, then Yin-Yang logic would make either the 5 pair on the left edge or the 6 pair on the right edge to be both Snake Pit clues, causing two snakes of the same size touching each other. Thus, the top 5 is a Sashigane clue. We can then use the same logic to make the 6 at R5C10 a Sashigane clue. Using the Yin-Yang rule, we can assign most of the edge cells and do some light Sashigane logic on them.

    The 6 at R10C1 is forced to go to R9C4 because of the 7 above it, which in turn makes the 5 at R10C4 go right. The 6 at R10C7 can't go left (It would be forced to loop back on itself) or right (leaving a 1 cell snake) now, so it goes up. The 5 must pair with the ?.

    Now we pull back and observe the whole grid. More specifically, the clue numbers. Unless paired with another number, each clue occupies the indicated amount of cells. We can thus sum up all the clues and see how many cells are used. Adding them all up (not counting the ?s) gives 107, more than the total 100 cells. This means that some clues must be paired. The possible pairs are the top 7s and the 6 at R6C6 going up or down. It's not enough to only have 6 pair or 7 pair since the sum won't be decreased enough, so they must both be paired (6 goes down).

    The 22 available cells in the southeast corner forces the paired 6’s to use R7C6, leaving behind a nook. Only the 6 at R6C10 can reach this nook as a Sashigane clue. Which leaves only one way to make the 3 and 7 regions. The 6 at R2C5 has no space to be a snake. Completing it forces some Sashigane logic. The 4 at R4C6 must be a snake. With all of our numbers paired up, and the upper right being at least a 6, we have exactly 100 cells accounted for, so all cells must connect to a given number or question mark, and the upper right ? is a 6. Using this logic, we can "pull" (in order): the 7 pair, the 3 (Sashigane), the 6 (must extend upwards since ? can’t come down), back to the 4 (going down), and finally the 7.

    Another similar chain of logic starts at the top left cell, which must connect to the 5 at R1C4 now. R2C1 now connects to R5C1. To avoid blocking the 5, the 7 at R9C1 must go right after squeezing through 5 and 3. The 3 at R8C3 cannot go right since it touches another snake with size 3, so it goes to the left, squeezing 7 and 5. The other 3 can't go up (it would leave a one-block region below). The rest should be deduced by making sure all clues have the correct lengths. Keep in mind that the 4 at R4C2 is NOT a snake since it would be isolated.

    Look-Air / FiveCells

    Let's consider the two pairs of 1 on the bottom. A pair of 1s can't be both FiveCells. It's also not possible for only one of them to be FiveCells. Thus, they're both Look-Air clues (for both of the pairs). Then, for any 3 on an edge, if it's being sandwiched between the edge and a 1 clue, then it can't be a Look-Air clue. We can therefore determine that such 3s on the left and right edge are both FiveCells clue. Using the Yin-Yang rule, we can determine that the whole top half edge belongs to FiveCells.

    The 3 on R5C2 is cornered by the 1 that's just been determined as FiveCells. It must be a FiveCells clue going down. The 1 below it must connect to the 3 on its left, causing the 1 below this 3 to be a Look-Air clue. The only way to complete this 3-1-3 clue is by going to the right. On the edge opposite of this side, the 1 on R7C10 can't be a FiveCells clue since it would clash with the 1 above it. Connecting the two sides makes the whole bottom edge Look-Air region. We can then extend the 3s. The 2 on R8C2 has no valid FiveCells shape. Making it a Look-Air clue.

    The 1 on R10C4 must have its shaded cell going above it since going right would cause the bottom-left block and bottom-right block staring at each other (A Look-Air 2 clue can never be shaded). This means the shaded cell for R10C6 must be on the bottom, forcing the bottom-right block to extend right. Now, let's check the 2 on R4C10. If it's a Look-Air clue, then it must be adjacent to 2 blocks of size 1. This would leave the 1 on R7C10 with no valid configuration. Thus, it's a FiveCells clue. This clue must connect to the 1 below it, expanding into a complete shape. The 2 above can also expand now. The 1s cornered by the regions must be Look-Air clues. The cell in R7C9 must connect to both of the 1’s adjacent to it to avoid a checkerboard pattern (which would eventually break the "connected parts" rule).

    If the 1 on R7C5 is a FiveCells clue, the 1 below it must be Look-Air. However, there's no place to place its shaded cell, causing contradiction. Due to the size 1 blocks on the left, the shaded cell for this 1 must go to its right. The size of this block must also be bigger than 1. If the 2 on R9C7 is a FiveCells clue, then the only shape it can have is a U. This would break the 1 clue on R8C5. Thus, we can be sure that it's a Look-Air clue and fill in some shaded cells accordingly. This would in turn determine that the cells to the left must belong to Look-Air. The 3 on R8C8 can't be FiveCells now since there's nowhere for it to go. We can thus shade in more cells

    The cornered 2 on R6C5 must be Look-Air, cornering another 2, then 1, into Look-Air. R5C5 is shaded because of the 2 on R6C5. The 3 on R5C3 must be FiveCells now. The 1 at R4C6 can’t be a FiveCells clue since it would run into the 1 at R4C4 or R4C8. Thus, this 1 must be Look-Air. If we try to make the cell to its left shaded, the shaded block will run into the 1 at R4C4. Therefore, its shaded cell goes above it, expanding into a size 2 block. If the 3 on R3C8 is FiveCells, then it must connect with the 2 on R2C10, which breaks the top edge. Thus it must be Look-Air. We can shade the valid cells around it and assign more FiveCells regions.

    There's only one way to resolve the 2 on R2C10 now. This causes a chain reaction for the top 2s. Due to the block on R8C3, the 2 on R2C2 can't be a Look-Air clue. There isn't a lot of space in here, so this 2 must connect to the 1 on R3C3, meaning that it can't go up or left. This means that R1C1 goes right and R2C1 goes down. The 3 can't connect to the cell above it now since it wouldn't lead to a valid shape. This leaves only one valid configuration for the rest of the cells.

    Heyawake / Aqre

    If the 3 region on R1C8 is Heyawake, then the 1 region can't be Heyawake, but that would create a trapped region inside this 3 region. Therefore, it's in Aqre. This, in turn, makes the 3 region below it also Aqre. The same logic can be used for the 3 region on R1C5, making the top right corner Aqre regions. Now, since we established where the Aqre cells must be, all Aqre cells must connect to it. Below, a red edge means that the Aqre connection can't go through it.

    As we see here, the only way to go from the bottom half to the top half is to go through the horizontal 2. This greatly limits the possibilities. The 1 region at R10C7 can't be Aqre now since the path would use up the cells in the 2 and 1 regions before reaching R7C7. Let's focus on the bottleneck at R7C5 next. R6C3 can't be a shaded Aqre cell since it can't reach the bottleneck. R7C3 also can't be a shaded Aqre cell since it must go directly to R7C5, which would eventually create 4 continuous blank cells. R8C3 also can't be a shaded Aqre cell since it'd need to go to the right, eventually causing R7C7 to be blocked. As a result of all of these blockages, the regions on the bottom left are all Heyawake regions. The regions being sandwiched would in turn be turned into Heyawake regions based on the Yin-Yang rule, which cause all blocked regions to be Heyawake regions.

    Trying to make the 2 on R6C6 a Heyawake region would result in the bottom right 1 being blocked. Therefore it's an Aqre region. Using the same reasoning, we can be sure that the shaded cells of the vertical 2 is at the top and the bottom, triggering a chain reaction at the left with Heyawake logic. The 1 region at R8C7 now must be Heyawake, filling in two more shaded cells. The 1 region at R7C7 can't be Heyawake now.

    If the 1 region at R6C9 is a Heyawake region, then both R9C9 and R10C8 must be shaded, which means that R10C9 can't escape, not even through R8C10 since that'd go against Heyawake's rule. Therefore, it's an Aqre region. The 1 region at R6C10 also can't be Heyawake now since its only path would be blocked by the horizontal 1 region. Due to connectivity, the bottoms of both 1 regions can't be shaded, making R7C10 shaded. The path must then go through R5C8.

    Using Aqre logic, we can construct a path upwards. Now, we need to check the 1 region at R1C7 since it's the only way to go between left and right. Out of the 3 possible options, only the middle one works since the bottom one directly go against Aqre's rule and the top one is too far. The resulting unshaded cells at R1 can let us shade in more cells.

    Due to R4C8 being unshaded, the 0 region to its left can't be Aqre. This let us shade in R5C2. We now put our focus on the regions of 1 at top left. If both of these regions are Heyawake, then there will be a rule violation at R3. If they're both Aqre, then the path can't work without running into 4 shaded cells in a row. Therefore, the left one is Heyawake, the right one is Aqre. We can now finish the Aqre path. We can also shade in R1C1 to avoid blocked space. The 0 region at R3C3 Must be Heyawake now, causing R4C4 to be shaded. R3C2 must belong to Aqre then.

    Balance Loop / Shikaku

    First, we focus on the 6 at R8C10. If we make it a Balance Loop clue, the long arm must go toward the top. This leaves no room for 5 to be either kind of clue. Thus, the 6 clue must be Shikaku. The 5 still doesn't have a valid way to be a Balance Loop clue. Therefore it's also a Shikaku clue. This would leave 6 to be a 2*3 area. The 5 below it must then be in Balance Loop. The long arm stretches into another 5. With no way to put an arm with length 2, this segment must be a length 5 horizontal line. We can then extend the loop using normal Balance Loop logic.

    We then check the 6 on R8C1. If it's a Shikaku clue, then by Yin-Yang logic, the 7 above it must also be a Shikaku clue. However, it would not have the space to expand. Thus, the 6 is a Balance Loop clue, and has its long portion extending upwards. The 7 still doesn't have enough space to expand as a Shikaku, therefore it's a Balance clue, sharing a line with the 6 clue. The line must bend on 7. The 7 at R7C2 must be in Balance Loop. The 4 at R2C2 can't be in Balance Loop since its long arm would go into 3. Any possible configuration of this 4, combined with the Yin-Yang rule, would make R1C4 and the cells to its right part of Shikaku. We can now fulfill the 4 at R1C9.

    We now go to the 7 at R7C2. Its long arm can be at most length 5, which means its short arm is at least length 2. Trying to go downwards results in the ? being out of the loop. Now we know that the 7 goes up at least 2 cells and goes right at least 3 cells. This encloses the bottom left corner. The 3 at R8C3 can't have its long arm go left (breaks the 6) or right (breaks the loop). We can use normal Balance loop logic to claim more cells. Notice that there must be a length 2 line extending upward from the ? clue, which forces the loop to turn at the 6, and travel two units to the right from the upper 7. The lower 7 now has to extend to the right by at least 4 units, hitting the 5

    The 3 at R8C8 can't be Balance Loop now since the configuration that doesn't immediately contradict runs a length 2 line into 4. Trying to make the 3 at R4C9 a Balance Loop clue means that the 6 near it is a Shikaku clue, causing the line to eventually be blocked by itself. Trying to make the 4 at R7C6 a Balance Loop clue would cause either the 6 to be blocked off or being blocked by a 5 clue. If this 4 extends upward, then R5C8 can't be a Shikaku cell. However, trying to escape as a Balance Loop cell would cause the 6 to be invalid. Therefore, it must only go to the left.

    Now, if the 6 at R3C8 is a Balance Loop clue, then the 5 at R5C4 would also need to be a Balance Loop clue. However, this would result in the 3 in the middle being isolated. Actually, the 5 at R5C4 can't be a Balance Loop clue at all. If its long arm goes up, then the 3s would seal off the whole area on the right side, leaving unclued Shikaku cells. If it goes right, then there would be an invalid 3 clue. We can thus fill in some cells to its right. A thin 6 region would eventually cause R5C9 to be isolated. We can now fill in the right half.

    If the 3 at R4C5 is a Shikaku clue, it'd block the loop. After extending out its arms, we can see that the loop must go up from R3C3, then do a right turn. The 3 at R2C5 can't be a Balance Loop clue now. The rest should be straightforward. Remember that the top left 4 needs to connect to other regions. Also the R6C3 cell can't be a Shikaku cell since no clue can reach it. However, it is allowed to be a blank Balance Loop clue.

    Ripple Effect / Islands

    First, we check the top left regions. These two 3 regions can't be the same type of region since the 3s are too close in either case. Next, we check the regions at R3C10 and R4C10. They can't be both Ripple Effects since that would make R3C10 unable to be filled. If the R3C10 region is in Islands, the 3 above it can't be in Islands. Using the Yin-Yang rule, we can be sure that the R4C10 region is in Islands. This would suggest that the R4C10 must be Islands regardless of what the R3C10 is. Moreover, the Yin-Yang connection cannot run through the top right 3 region, making the type assignments on the edge clear.

    Now, we focus on the bottom left. The three adjacent regions must have at least 6 shaded cells. Since the shaded cells can't be adjacent across a thick border, we can only place at most 6 shaded cells here (3 horizontal pair + 1 vertical pair + 2 free cells). This would mean that these regions get 1, 2, and 3 shaded cells in some order. This would also mean that R7C2 and R10C4 must be shaded. Since the shaded cells must be continuous in a region, R9C1 and R8C3 can't be shaded. This. in turn, would make R1C10 and R8C2 shaded. R10C3 is also shaded since we don't have enough space. We can now do some normal Islands logic to get the following grid:

    We now move to the region at R8C7. It can't be in Islands now. The region at R8C8 also can't be in Islands since that would cause the T-shaped region above it to run out of choices. These regions escape through the 2 region above them. We can now fill in the numbers. The 3 at R7C8 means that the 3 region above it is in Islands, solidifying the right side of the Islands grid. Connectivity means that the 1 region at R5C7 is also part of Ripple Effect. We can fill in some numbers in the top right regions.

    If the region at R6C4 is also in Ripple Effect, it would cause the region to its right to have no place for any 2. This means that it's in Islands, disambiguating the bottom left. The 1 at R6C2 is adjacent to an Islands region with one island, so this clue must be part of Ripple Effect. Connectivity means the rest of the region are all Ripple Effect regions. This also means that the Islands part of the puzzle is done.

    Using the region at R3C5, we fill in a chain of 2s. The region at R1C3 can help us fill in more numbers. Note that we can fill in a 3 at R7C3. Now, we check the region at R1C5. A 3 at R2C5 would force two 3s on R3 to touch. The resulting choices of 3 means that R5C4 can't be a 3, forcing R5C2 to be a 3.

    A 4 at R3C7 would force the positions of the 3s, causing two 5s at R2. Knowing that the 4 is at R2C6, the rest of the top regions can be solved. Look at the region at R4C5 now. The 3 can only be in a cell in C6. This causes the bottom region to only have one valid cell (R6C5) for 3. We can then get the 4, then the 5. The rest should be straightforward. Notice that the 5 at R3C8 causes R3C3 to be 1.
