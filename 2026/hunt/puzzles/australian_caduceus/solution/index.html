    Solution to Australian Caduceus

    Answer: BATTLING OF THE NAGAS
    Written by: Max Chang
    Edited by: Dan Simon and Benji Nguyen

    This puzzle is themed after the children's board game Snakes & Ladders.

    As clued by the title and flavor text, the serpents and wires are twisted together in pairs. Matching the length of the Puzzmon’s name and the length of the wire segment reveals the correct pairing. Using the Australian standard 20-pair colour code, each serpent-wire pair can be mapped to a particular number between 1 and 100; the same numbers that appear on Snakes & Ladder board. Solvers should hypothesize that the number is either the start or end of the serpent and the wire segments indicate the path of the serpent. Since some of the serpent-wire pairs clue numbers between 1 and 10, the bottom row of the Snakes & Ladder board, and all of the wire segments are at least 2 squares tall, you can rule out the number representing the head of the serpent.


    Puzzmon NameWire ColorPuzzmon ColorTail Square #
    BunboaWhiteBrown4
    TtattellWhiteBlue and brown9
    NimagYellowOrange and green32
    OudinenuffYellowGrey and white40
    TehBlackOrange42
    AgnhissPurpleGreen and white75
    AzugRedBlue and orange87


    The area of all 8 ladder segments sum up to 100 which is the same as the area of the Snakes & Ladder board. A reasonable hypothesis is that the ladder pieces must cover the entire board without overlapping.

    Using the ladder pieces and serpent-wire pairs, teams must reconstruct the Snakes & Ladders board such that you can proceed through the game from start to finish rolling only 1s (as clued by the dice with only single pips) while climbing every ladder and sliding down every serpent.

    There is only one configuration of the ladders that satisfy all of these conditions. There are many logical paths that you can use to determine the proper configuration. The most helpful realization is that, in order to avoid getting stuck in an endless loop, you cannot step on the same square twice. Therefore, because you must ascend each ladder and descend each serpent, you cannot step through the tail of a serpent or the top of a ladder from the previously numbered cell.

    One possible solve path is to start with Oudinenuff and Teh that have tails at 40 and 42 respectively. Somewhere along the correct path you must descend Oudinenuff and land at 40. If you walk forward 2 squares from 40 to 42 without landing on the base of a ladder, then you will land on the tail of Teh which violates the conclusion that you cannot step through the tail of a serpent from the previously numbered cell. Therefore, either square 40 or 41 must have the base of a ladder. The only possible ladder that can fit here is the ladder on the 6 (rows) by 3 (columns) piece. If the base of that ladder was on square 40, then the ladder would just take you to square 41 which doesn't resolve the issue. Therefore, we know the base of this ladder must be on square 41 and take you to square 60.



    With this piece placed, let's move our attention to the 3 (rows) by 7 (columns) piece and the 7 (rows) by 2 (columns) piece. The only way for both of these pieces to fit on the board is for the 3 by 7 piece to either be in the top right or bottom right corner. If we put it in the bottom right corner, then we must put the 2 (rows) by 3 (columns) piece in the bottom left corner. With these two piece placements, there are ladders that start at square 2 and square 10 but no ladders in between. This is problematic when you think about what happens after you descend Bunboa. When you descend Bunboa, you land on square 4 and then walk one square at a time until you reach the base of the ladder on square 10. Along this path, you must walk through square 9 which is the tail of Ttattell which violates the previous conclusion. Therefore, the 3 by 7 piece must be placed in the top right corner and the 2 by 3 piece must be placed in the top left corner.



    The only piece that can be placed in the bottom left corner is the 2 (rows) by 5 (columns) piece. Then, we must place the 5 (rows) by 2 (columns) piece directly to the right of the 6 by 3 piece.



    There are 3 pieces remaining. If the 7 (rows) by 2 (columns) piece was placed on the left side of the remaining empty area, then its ladder would go from square 7 to square 55. After ascending that ladder, the player would walk over square 60, the top of another ladder, which would create a cycle. Therefore, this piece must be on the right side of the remaining empty area.



    Lastly, in order to get to the ladder starting at square 71, there must be a ladder that ends after Oudinenuff's head (between squares 66 and 71 inclusive). Therefore, the 3 (rows) by 3 (columns) piece must be placed at the top of the remaining empty region with the last piece fitting in below it.



    Starting from square 1, the path to square 100 would be:


    • Walk from 1 to 3
    • Climb the ladder from 3 to 18
    • Walk from 18 to 21
    • Slide down BUNBOA from 21 to 4
    • Walk from 4 to 7
    • Climb the ladder from 7 to 27
    • Walk from 27 to 28
    • Slide down TTATTELL from 28 to 9
    • Walk from 9 to 10
    • Climb the ladder from 10 to 52
    • Walk from 52 to 54
    • Slide down NIMAG from 54 to 32
    • Walk from 32 to 36
    • Climb the ladder from 36 to 64
    • Slide down OUDINENUFF from 65 to 40
    • Walk from 40 to 41
    • Climb the ladder from 41 to 60
    • Walk from 60 to 62
    • Slide down TEH from 62 to 42
    • Walk from 42 to 47
    • Climb the ladder from 47 to 68
    • Walk from 68 to 71
    • Climb the ladder from 71 to 92
    • Walk from 92 to 96
    • Slide down AZUG from 96 to 87
    • Walk from 87 to 89
    • Slide down AGNHISS from 89 to 75
    • Walk from 75 to 82
    • Climb the ladder from 82 to 98
    • Walk from 98 to 100

    If you write the name of each serpent along its path and take the letters you walk (not slide or climb) through in order, you get the final answer, BATTLING OF THE NAGAS.


    Author's Notes

    Snakes and Ladders is not a particularly good game, but seemed like a good theme for a puzzle that could leverage the colors of our serpent Puzzmon.
