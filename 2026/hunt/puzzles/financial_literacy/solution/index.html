    Solution to Financial Literacy

    Answer: SOLDIER
    Written by: Jenna Himawan
    Edited by: Benji Nguyen
    Additional thanks to:
    • sovannjet lim for taking photos

    Math Questions

    We begin by filling in the blanks in the math questions.


    1. Misfit toys can be hard to sell. Each toy depreciates by $_ per year, so toys that are initially worth $45 will be worth $0 after 9 years. If you have to pay a storage cost of $_ per year, you can sell a toy within 5 years to break even or better (assuming that you initially acquired a 45-dollar toy for free).

    x * (9 years) = $45; x = $5/year

    ($45 - ($5/year) * 5 years) - y * (5 years) >= $0; y <= $4/year


    1. Lost wages require compensation. If the revenue from one worker is $_ per minute, then every hour they don’t work means $180 in lost revenue. If a worker gets injured and you need to pay them $_ per minute for the time they cannot work, the total loss from worker injury is $300 per hour.

    $180 / hr = x * (60 min / hr); x = $3 / min

    $300 / hr = $180 / hr + y * (60 min / hr); y = $2 / min


    1. Dream jobs can become real jobs! Let’s say that you pay $3 per year in tuition to MIT for 4 years, but after graduating, you’ll make $_ per year. If you didn’t attend MIT, you would have made $2 per year for those 4 years and for the rest of your career. 10 years after graduating, you’ll make more money having gone to MIT than if you hadn’t gone to MIT. Now let’s assume that you received $_ per year from a scholarship. This would allow you to break even 6 years after graduating.

    x * (10 yr) - ($3 / yr) * 4 yr = ($2 / yr) * (14 yr); x = $4 / yr

    ($4 / yr) * (6 yr) - ($3 / yr - y) * 4 yr = ($2 / yr) * (10 yr); y = $2 / yr


    1. Park your car legally... most of the time! If it takes you an average of 4 minutes to make a delivery and you’re paid $15 per hour, this means you’re paid $_ per delivery. Now assume that for each delivery, you can park illegally and reduce your delivery time to 1 minute, keeping the same rate of pay per delivery (not per hour). If you do so, you risk getting a parking ticket with a probability of 15% (you will still make your delivery and be paid for it). The penalty from a single ticket would have to be less than $_ for the illegal parking to be profitable.

    x * (1 delivery / 4 min) * (60 min / hr) = ($15 / hr); x = $1 / delivery

    4 deliveries * ($1 / delivery) - 4 (0.15 * y) >= $1; y <= $5


    1. Kangaroo rats are eating your crops! Let’s say you want to keep the kangaroo rats away from your crops. You have a 40 mile x 40 mile square plot of land. If you pay $160 for a fence to completely surround your property, that means you pay a rate of $_ per mile for fencing. Let’s say you would ordinarily lose 25% of your yearly crop to the kangaroo rats, and after the fence is built, you lose 10%. The crops are worth $_ per square mile, assuming no losses. The net profit from the fence after one year would be $80.

    $160 / (40 mi * 4) = $1 / mi = x

    (y (40 mi * 40 mi) * (1 - 0.10) - $160) - y (40 mi * 40 mi) * (1 - 0.25) = $80; y = $1 / (mi * mi)


    1. Special attention should be paid to multiplicative factors! Account 1 has $7. Account 2 has $1. At the end of every year, the amount in each account increases by $1. In _ years, the former will have 3 times as much money as the latter. _ years after that, the former will have 2 times as much money as the latter.

    $7 + x * ($1 / yr) = 3($1 + x * ($1 / yr)); x = 2 yr

    $7 + (2 yr + y) * ($1 / yr) = 2($1 + (2 yr + y) * ($1 / yr)); y = 3 yr


    1. Grunt work is no fun! If you have 2,520 person-hours of work, if you have _ people working on it, 8 hours a day, 5 days a week, it will take you 9 weeks to complete the task. If you need to complete the task in _ weeks, you should hire 21 people.

    2520 person * hr = x * (9 wk) * (5 day / wk) * (8 hr / day); x = 7 people

    2520 person * hr = 21 people * (5 day / wk) * (8 hr / day) * y; y = 3 weeks


    1. Respect from your colleagues is important. You decide to start bringing coffee in for your coworkers. If picking up the coffee adds 1.5 extra miles to your commute and you walk at _ miles per hour, your commute will take an extra 30 minutes. If you charge your coworkers 50 cents per trip, you would be paid at a rate of $_ per hour.

    (1.5 mi) * (60 min / hr) = (30 min) * x; x = 3 mi / hr

    50 cents * ($1 / 100 cents) / ((30 min) * (1 hr / 60 min)) = $1 / hr = y


    1. Lady beetles are an excellent way to get rid of aphids. You start a business selling lady beetles. Assuming that you start with 2 beetles and that the beetle population doubles every month, after _ full months, you will have 128 beetles. If feeding 1 beetle for 1 month costs $_, feeding your beetles over this time period will cost you $254.

    2 * 2^x = 128; x = 6

    254 = (2 + 4 + 8 + 16 + 32 + 64 + 128) * y; y = 1


    1. A thousand dollars is a lot of money! Assume you put $1000 into a savings account with an interest rate of _% per year, compounded annually. After 2 years, you’ll have $1,166.40. After _ years, you’ll have $1,586.87.

    1000 * (1 + (x / 100))^2 = 1166.40; x = 8

    1000 * (1 + 0.08)^y = 1586.87; y = 6


    1. Fire insurance mitigates financial uncertainty. If your property is worth 75 thousand dollars and has a 4% chance of being destroyed by a fire this year, the insurance company should charge you _ thousand dollars this year to expect to break even. If your property value doubles, and fire risk goes up 2 percentage points, the insurance company should charge you _ thousand dollars per year.

    75 thousand dollars * 0.04 = 3 thousand dollars = x

    (75 thousand dollars) * 2 * (0.04 + 0.02) = 9 thousand dollars = y


    1. Skipjack tuna can be bought in groups of 4 for $16, at a discount of 20% compared to buying them individually. If you spend $63, the maximum number of tuna you can buy is _. The price of an individual tuna is $.

    $16 = (1 - 0.2) * 4 * y; y = $5

    4 tuna * floor(63/16) + $(63 % 16) / ($5 / tuna) = 15 tuna = x

    y = $5, as we found earlier.


    1. Polity is difficult to maintain. You’re the president of a small country with a budget of _ thousand dollars per year. You obtain this money through an income tax of 2%, when your citizens make $50,000 per year in total. If you raise this income tax to 8% (assuming no changes to your citizens’ total income), you would have _ thousand dollars per year.

    50000 * 0.02 = $1 thousand / yr = x

    50000 * 0.08 = $4 thousand / yr = y

    Putting it together

    Using the numbers from the math problems to index into the correctly-ordered blanks from the Nebula award winners gives a cluephrase: INFINITE STARS DARK FRONTIERS.

    Infinite Stars: Dark Frontiers is a space opera anthology. Amazon’s summary says: This space opera anthology features new stories from popular writers and essential works from past masters, including 26 new stories from series like...

    If we take a look at the table of contents, we’ll see that the first words of the short story titles also appear in the first words of the math problems. Many of the math problems start in a weird way, which helps us confirm the theory that the space opera anthology is important. (Note: Online sources don’t report the table of contents very well. We consider the table of contents, as printed in the book, to be canonical; use the Google Books or Amazon preview.)

    We associate the math problems with the corresponding short stories, find the position of each story in the collection, and use A1Z26.

    Author’s Notes

    Identifying where the images are taken is not strictly necessary for the solve. To identify the first location, you can search for "financial boot camp IAP MIT"; this gives results for MIT Student Financial Services' event listing (Happy Lamb Hot Pot is also on this event listing). For the other image, you may search for "include note with name when returning books MIT"; this will give a result for the MIT Science Fiction Society. These locations can be further confirmed by the "topic" of each quiz, the SFS initials, the title of the puzzle, or actually going to those locations in person.

    I’m very happy that I was able to combine all these sources of inspiration into one puzzle:

    • Shortly after I arrived at MIT, I tried to email the MIT Science Fiction Society (mitsfs@mit.edu) about their catalog. I instead emailed Student Financial Services (sfs@mit.edu), and received a polite but confused response.
    • I read the Infinite Stars: Dark Frontiers anthology several years ago and was delighted that there were 26 stories in it.
    • I’ve thought for a while that Nebula Awards would be a fun data set, but I’ve never had a chance to use it.
