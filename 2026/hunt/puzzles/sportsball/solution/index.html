    All 4 commissioners have different understandings of which teams will be in the Sportsball championship. As clued by the flavor text, the playoff structures for each of the original leagues are important. The first realization to make is that each commissioner is applying their league's playoff structure to the Sportsball league and that explains why they are each coming to different conclusions about the outcome. To solve this puzzle, you will need to dig into the playoff seeding, structure, and tiebreaking logic for the MLB, NBA, NHL, and NFL. This information can be found on Wikipedia.

    The 24 teams in the Sportsball league each come from one of the 4 leagues with 6 teams from each league. Identify the 24 teams and which league they come from by their team logos.

    Looking at the teams' statements, you will also notice that there are multiple teams claiming to be the same seed and some teams claiming things that aren't compatible with some playoff structures (e.g. The NHL playoffs don't have any team seeded 5th or 6th and only the NBA has a play-in). However, each team's statement is compatible with their league's playoff structure (e.g. No NHL team makes a statement about being seeded 5th or 6th and only NBA teams make statements referencing the play-in). You should come to the conclusion that each team is also applying their league's playoff structure to the Sportsball league and their statements reflect that playoff structure.

    The next step is to use the statements from the commissioners and the teams to figure out the week 34 results such that all of their statements accurately reflects their perspective of the playoff bracket. An example of the logic needed to figure out the scores can be found below.

    Note: In the Sportsball league, the teams are grouped into conferences and divisions. These conferences and divisions are used in the tiebreaking rules and bracket structure for each of the sports leagues. The names of the conferences and divisions clue how they should be interpreted within each league's rules. For example, the Western American League conference should be interpreted as the Western Conference for the NBA and NHL, the American League for the MLB, and American Football Conference for the NFL. The division names are a concatenation of division names from each of the 4 leagues in the order NFL, NHL, NBA, MLB. For example, team 7's division is South-Central-Pacific-East so it belongs to the NFL's South division, NHL's Central division, NBA's Pacific division, and MLB's East division.

    Once you have determined all of the week 34 results, index each team's week 34 score into their team name to spell the message INDEX SEED INTO CHAMP TROPHY. This clue phrase is telling you to index each bracket's champion into that league's championship trophy name. If you haven't already, you need to figure out the seeds and brackets for each of the four leagues. Once the bracket is set up, the Secret Sportsball Postseason & Play-in Script is used to walk through the playoffs and identify who the champion would be. Taking the seeds of each of those champions in the order their commissioners speak in the puzzle and indexing into the name of the league's trophy gets you the final answer, MOTE.

    In the NFL, 7 teams make the playoffs from each conference. The winners of each of the 4 divisions in the conference are guaranteed to be seeds 1 through 4, ordered according to the seeding and tiebreak rules. Then the top 3 remaining teams are seeded 5 through 7, again ordered by the seeding and tiebreak rules. We can start by identifying some teams that cannot make the playoffs. In the East division of the American Football Conference (AFC), the three teams (Team 5, 8, and 9) have 17 wins, 16 wins, and 16 wins prior to week 34. This means that after week 34, the two teams that do not win that division must each have at least 16 wins. The runner-up of the West division of the AFC is also guaranteed to have at least 16 wins. Therefore, in order to be a wild card team in the AFC, you must have at least 16 wins. This means that any team that only has 14 wins before week 34 cannot possibly be a wild card team. If they are also not within one win of the current leader of their division, they also cannot win their division and therefore cannot make the playoffs. This allows us to mark Team 10 as NA since it has only 14 wins and is 5 games behind Team 11 in the South division of the AFC. By a similar line of reasoning, we can also mark Teams 16, 19, and 22 as NA in the National Football Conference (NFC).

    Let's look at some specific divisions. In the AFC's South division, Team 11 leads by more than 1 win prior to week 34. Therefore, after week 34, it is still guaranteed to be leading the division. We can mark Team 11 as the AFC South Winner. We can apply the same logic to Teams 13 and 23 in the NFC. We also know that Team 14 is a 4 seed which, according to the NFL seeding rules, means it must have won its division. Going into week 34, Team 17 has a one-game lead on Team 14 in the NFC West which means that Team 14 must have beaten Team 17 in their week 34 matchup against one another to give them both a 17-17 record with Team 14 winning the tiebreakers. (In the NFL tiebreakers, Team 14 and 17 split their head to head and they both have a 2-2 NFC West division record. Since Team 17 only has 17 wins after week 34, it cannot be a wild card team either.

    Author's Notes

    I felt that there was a dearth of sports puzzles in our hunt so I used one of the puzzle slots in The Glitch round for this idea that I've had in the back of my mind for a while. Unfortunately, getting playtesters for this puzzle was difficult because Cardinality is quite lacking in sports enthusiasts (or even sports tolerators).

    The original idea for this puzzle came from a puzzle book that I did as a child titled Puzzles for Pleasure by E.R. Emmet. The book had a chapter of logic puzzles that were presented as tables of hockey league standings with columns like "Games Won", "Games Lost", "Points For", "Points Against", etc. Each of the tables had some twist: either there were missing values or some values were off by one due to teams misreporting results. The goal of each puzzle was to figure out the true scores of each game given the conditions. It was my first introduction to puzzles and the topic of one of my college admissions essays.

    An earlier draft of this puzzle incorporated the four major US sports leagues and the CFL (Canadian Football League) and AFL (Australian Football League). Both of these leagues have a unique playoff property that made them interesting for the puzzle but we wanted to reduce the solve time so unfortunately, they did not make the cut. The Canadian Football league has a crossover rule that allows the fourth place team in one division to replace the third place team in the other division if it has a strictly better record. The Australian Football League uses point ratios instead of point differential as a tiebreaker.
