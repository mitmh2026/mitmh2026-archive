    Solution to V-fun

    Answer: BOWL OVER
    Written by: Andy Chen and Jerry Chen
    Edited by: Max Chang and Bryce Cai

    We are presented with two parts:

    • A list of crossword-like clues (but with no enumerations), split by Across and Down and grouped in sets of 2-5. Eight of the answers are of the form "Vn (F. Lastname)"
    • A set of flavortext, instructions, and eight numbered grid-like diagrams that show rock climbs. These are Kilter Board Layouts, though knowing this is not necessary for any part of the puzzle.

    We first try to solve as many of the crossword clues as possible, noting that without additional context, there might be some ambiguity. We might want to put these clues in some kind of 2D crossword grid — but which one? We might observe some of the following:

    • The Kilter Board climbs at the bottom each have 19 rows and 17 columns of “large” holds, as well as some interspersed smaller footholds.
    • The crossword clues form 19 groups of across clues and 17 groups of down clues.
    • The total number of letters in each across group is roughly 17, depending on our answers at this stage. Similarly, the total number of letters in each down group is roughly 19.

    This information implies that the crossword answers form a dense 19x17 letter grid, and in each row or column, the answers are concatenated in the given order. Constructing the grid helps resolve any answer ambiguity and reveals the answers to the Vn clues.

    Full Grid with Highlighted Vn Clues

    We can then discover that the Vn answers are names of famous outdoor boulders. The number in the clue corresponds to the boulder’s V-grade (a measure of difficulty). The Vn clue answers, in order of appearance in the clue list, are the following:

    We can then turn our attention to the second part of the puzzle, noting that there are eight boulder names and eight Kilter Board grids. This observation implies that each Kilter Board grid represents one of the outdoor boulders — or the moves on that boulder.

    The name in each Vn clue represents a climber who has ascended the boulder. In fact, each climber’s ascent is so noteworthy that it’s documented on YouTube. This video footage removes ambiguity in the sequence of moves, so it’s easier to figure out the mapping between Kilter Board grid and outdoor boulder.

    We may then wonder what new information the mapping provides. The italicized flavortext implies the final extraction step: it turns out every Kilter Board grid has exactly one (large) hold that overlaps with its boulder name.

    Kilter Board Grid 2 — overlapped letter O
    Kilter Board Grid 3 — overlapped letter W
    Kilter Board Grid 4 — overlapped letter L

    Reading the overlapping letters in order of the Kilter Board grids yields the final answer of BOWL OVER.


    Author's Notes (Andy):

    I’d thought about trying to write a climbing-themed puzzle for a while. The 2024 hunt featured a competition climbing puzzle (Inaugural Funky Stickperson Contest), so I wanted to focus on outdoor boulders.

    Originally, Jerry and I honed in on the silly names given to boulders, making rebus puzzles that clued their names. Later, though, we realized that the Kilter Board layout could serve as a vehicle for a letter grid. We also realized that folks often replicated outdoor climbs on Kilter Board, which inspired the final puzzle mechanism.

    The crux of the puzzle construction was definitely the dense letter grid. It turned out that grouping the boulder names in pairs of consecutive columns created the most relaxed constraints, as then I could focus on satisfying bigrams instead of worrying about potentially impossible trigrams. Some of the answer lengths were shorter than I hoped, but I was glad to have a complete construction at all.

    Another challenge was that each boulder might have a different "beta" (sequence of hand and foot moves) depending on the person. We chose boulders that had sufficiently distinct features so that folks could more easily distinguish the boulders.

    A big thank you goes to the folks running Kilter Lookup (a site for searching Kilter Board climbs); they helped us with custom requests in early puzzle iterations. Credit also goes to the following Kilter Board users who designed outdoor boulder replicas that heavily inspired the puzzle’s grids:

    • Lwjma — Floatin
    • EMPA (Emil Abrahamsson) — Off the Wagon
