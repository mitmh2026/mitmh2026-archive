    Sam: So let's go over the hands, starting at board 1. We got a small pick-up here. This is why it pays not to open 1NT with a 5-card major!
    Esther: Are you sure that's good advice? Didn't you just get lucky that your opponents didn't balance?
    That's potato, potato.
    On to board 2. We got a good score because the opponents followed the rule of 15.
    At our table we ignored it. I'm not sure if that was right, but hey, can't argue with results.
    We wrong-sided our contract and they found the right lead, but still, at least we got a positive score.
    Next board, the auction was standard enough: standard opening, standard invitational sequence, standard decline. It looks like we won it in the declarer play.
    Partner dropped the 9 on the first round of trump and it tricked them.
    Did they! Nice job. They probably thought we weren't good enough to find that. I wasn't fooled, I made my contract.
    Well done.
    And now starting with board 4 come the bad results.
    They found a good sacrifice against us at our table.
    Right. At our table we pushed them up one level, but that wasn't enough. We should have tried again!
    I guess the five level really does belong to our opponents.
    Don't tell me we lost board 5. We had a game forcing auction, learned we had one suit wide open, played game in our best fit, and got doubled. Typical. They even found the right lead, somehow.
    Our opponents just blasted 3NT.
    Victory again for 'just blasting'!
    Why on earth did you double on the next one?
    Uh, don't look at me. I guess my partner thought that with all his high card points there was no way they could make it.
    Their hand was terrible! And then the double told them exactly how to play the hand. Without it, we were lucky to only go down 1 at our table.
    Let's talk about the next one. Did you forget to hold up?
    We had a carding misunderstanding. I thought we were playing standard, partner thought we were playing upside-down.
    Not great, you gave up some overtricks.
    Overtricks are nothing compared to the disaster on board 8.
    Eesh. You can see why I doubled though, right? I have an amazing hand!
    I don't know how they bid a slam here. I stopped in game like a normal person. They're crazy!
    Really crazy. Let's go over board 9. It looks like we lost this one on the lead. Lead your longest and strongest suit--so much for that!
    Yeah, our opponents just led passively on this one and that was the winner.
    Argh, I'm so mad I went down on board 10.
    It's a textbook devil's coup, but I've never even seen one in real life...at least, not until my opponents pulled it off today. Have you?
    By God, no, of course not.
    Board 11 was another disaster.
    I see we really aren't making good doubling decisions against slams. We double when we should pass, we pass when we should double.
    The opponents told me to google Lightner doubles after this hand. Bit rude if you ask me but I guess they're right. My bad. It even cost an overtrick.
    Let's do the last one quickly. Board 12, they did well to penalize me. That was unpleasant.
    My partner saw their trump void and decided to pull my penalty double. I was so mad...
    It seems pretty reasonable to me, that hand looks very offense-oriented. Unfortunately nobody was making anything here.
    The final total is in. We won the first three boards and lost the other nine. What does that make it?
    Looks like we lost by 99 IMPs.
    Yikes! Lot of doubling, I count seven doubled contracts in total across our two tables. But hey, have you seen? It looks like our scorecard spells out something!
