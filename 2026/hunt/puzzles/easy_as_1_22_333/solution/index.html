    Solution to Easy as 1-22-333

    Answer: CENTERS
    Written by: Matt Koutsoutis and Annie Guo
    Edited by: Herman Chau
    Additional thanks to:
    • Cindy Jiang for helping write the shell and quests

    We begin by observing that many of these pieces can fit together like a jigsaw. The easiest way to do so is by shape, using the lines to help.

    Click to see clues used in this puzzle

    For instance, we can observe that the lines on the 3rd and 6th pieces in the second row match up perfectly. Continuing to assemble our jigsaw pieces yields four 8x8 grids:

    As clued by the title ("1-22-333") and confirmed by several of the quests, we want to fill in the grids with numbers, in particular as a Fillomino puzzle: each number N must be part of a continuous block of N cells containing N, and no two regions of the same number N can share an edge. We fill in each piece, forming almost complete 8x8 Fillominos, which allows us to uniquely assemble a 16x16 grid (as given by the shell) which is a valid 16x16 Fillomino.

    Now we follow the steps in the shell. We either ignore, take the number in a cell (single circle), or double the number in a cell (double circle). Then we draw regions according to the Fillomino and place the digits 1-8.

    We can deduce which rule applies to which region:

    1. The Nabner rule implies that at most 4 digits can be on each line, since we cannot have adjacent digits, e.g. 1357 is allowed, but 2357 is not allowed. So this must be the bottom right quadrant.
    2. The Renban rule implies that at most 8 digits can be on each line. Since we have assigned the bottom right quadrant, this must be the top right quadrant.
    3. The German Whispers rule implies that no number on the line can be a 4 or 5, since nothing differs by 5 from those numbers. Thus this cannot be the bottom left, where a line spans an entire row (thus including a 4 and 5), so it must be the top left quadrant.
    4. By process of elimination, the Region Sum rule must apply to the bottom left quadrant.

    Now we solve, where our circled and doubly circled numbers are our givens. We simultaneously draw our new regions, which adhere to Fillomino, Sudoku, and red line rules.

    As a pointer, German Whispers and Region Sum require relying more on keeping the original Fillomino pieces together and remapping them.

    Towards the end of our solve, notice that some regions are letter-shaped (highlighted above). Reading these regions yields the answer, CENTERS.
