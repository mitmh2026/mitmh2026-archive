    Solution to Doubles Games

    Answer: NIGHTHAWKS
    Written by: Kevin Der and Leonard Wei
    Edited by: Herman Chau and Dan Simon
    Additional thanks to:
    • Ryan Liu for initial implementation

    Overview

    There are 5 levels in this puzzle, each with distinct tiles and theming. As the title of the entire region is "Doubles Games", we can deduce that each one is a mashup of two different board games that incorporate hexagonal tiles. Each level entails placing tiles in order to satisfy constraints from the games' rules as well as various other facts that are given.

    Levels

    Going from east to west, north to south, the levels are:

    The Palace and Its Cats

    The tile art, as well as clues from the flavortext such as "Maria of Aragon" and the mention of cats, leads us to the games Azul: Queen's Garden and Calico.

    Click to view detailed solution The tile art consists of the Calico tile colors, and the structures and patterns from Azul. In addition, tooltips on certain tiles mention button tokens, or attracting a cat, which imply certain constraints about a given tile and any groups (of adjacent colors or patterns) that it is a part of. Many of the garden structures also display statements like "3-2-1 both", which are Calico scoring objectives that describe the tiles that surround that structure.

    In particular, some of the constraints we need to understand are:

    • in Azul, no two tiles in a connected group may have both the same color and pattern (i.e. no duplicate tiles)
    • a button token means the tile is part of a group of tiles of the same color
    • attracting a cat means the tile is part of a group of a certain size or shape where all have the same pattern
      • "13th century Persian poet" refers to the Calico cat called Rumi, which is attracted by 3 tiles in a row
      • "Spanish owl" refers to Tecolote, which is attracted by 4 tiles in a row
      • "tree nut found in frangipane" refers to Almond, which is attracted by 5 tiles in a trapezoidal shape
    • "3-2-1 both" for example means that of the six tiles surrounding that structure, there are 3 of one kind, 2 of another, and 1 of another, in terms of both color and pattern
    • similarly, "≠ both" means that none of the surrounding six tiles share a color or a pattern

    By reconciling these constraints, all the tiles can be placed correctly. Some helpful places to start:

    • there are two tiles that can only be adjacent to both a pavilion and a bench, and these can be placed immediately in one of two possible locations
    • the two tiles that attract Almond can only go in two possible trapezoidal open spaces, which means all those tiles must have a certain pattern
    • a similar deduction can be made for the tile that attracts Tecolote
    • several tiles are adjacent to no structures, and there are only four such possible locations
    • tiles that are in a group of 5 or 6 that earn a button imply color constraints for a number of tiles

    From Jungle to Metropolis

    The flavor for this level directly quotes the rulebook descriptions of the games Landmarks and Suburbia. A search for many of the color tile names will also point to Suburbia.

    Click to view detailed solution We can see that the color tiles are city-building tiles from Suburbia. Similarly, the beige tiles with a single underlined word suggest the clue tiles from Landmarks. By observing the tiles that are already placed, we can gather that a clue tile must semantically relate to all of its neighbors (which in this case are city-building tiles). Similarly, many of the Suburbia tiles display adjacency bonuses, which when combined with the card's text in the game, yields constraints about the number of neighbor tiles of various colors.

    To place all the tiles correctly, some helpful starting points are:

    • the Casino is adjacent to a dealership and is suggestive of SPIN (a roulette wheel or a slot machine) so it must go in the only slot adjacent to the two.

    • BOARD is a tile that requires four adjacencies and relates to Homeowner's Association (an HOA board), Chip Fabrication Plant (circuit board), and Bed & Breakfast (room and board), so it must go on that curse.     Sunshine East of Eden

    The flavor text about light to help with the creation of sugars suggests Photosynthesis, and the sunshine on the east side of the level confirms it. The other game looks like it might be Ark Nova, since it's talking about a place filled with animals. To avoid this trap, we've been told the game is from 2025, not 2021, so we land on Sanctuary, a safe place further confirmed by our level title.

    Click to view detailed solution We can start by thinking about Photosynthesis. The title suggests that the sunshine won't move, it's just going to stay to the east. We are supposed to plant some trees in Open Areas, which ties together the two games. We know that all our tree tiles need to get light. The constraints we have:

    • A tree casts a shadow (to the west for us) based on how big it is. Big trees cast a shadow 3 spaces, medium ones 2 spaces, and small ones 1 space.
    • Bigger trees aren't shadowed by shadows cast by smaller trees.
    • All the tree tiles have white backgrounds, matching the fact that no trees are in shade.
    • Some of the other tiles have gray backgrounds -- they need to be in shade cast by trees. The white tiles cannot be in shade.

    Going to Sanctuary rules, we notice that our level is a very close approximation of the player board, including the entrance, bonus icons, and preplaced Open Areas (with trees). From the game rules, we have the following constraints:

    • We have to place the tiles (in the order provided) such that each tile placed is adjacent to a placed tile or the entrance.
    • Building tiles have additional placement constraints on the tile themselves, which must be satisfied at the time of placement.
    • Certain tiles have Open Area constraints, which also allows them to be placed.
    • Certain tooltips reference Upgrades, which require a particular condition to be achieved.

    This also explains the awkward orientation of the tiles -- the entire dimension has flat sides on the east and west edges, so locally this level has been rotated to match. The solution will discuss everything from the global orientation going forwards.

    For those interested, this level accurately describes 7 rounds of a solo Sanctuary game that ends up filling this board, including action strength, upgrades, and picking up enough tiles in hand. Since players start with zero tiles in hand, the first round is any animal action in order to draw two tiles. It is unnecessary to track all of this to solve the level.

    A reasonable solve path for the logic constraints:

    • Start by noticing that the Pavilions and the Zoo School need to be by the river. This forces the Europe Pavilion and the Zoo School to be by the southern river and uses up all the available space.
      • This means either the tree or the White Stork needs to be southeast of the entrance tile. By shading constraints, it must be the White Stork.
      • The Zoo School and the required Open Area to the east force the unique placement of the tiles by the southern river. The Zoo School also provides one of the two conservation markers needed later.
    • The Asia Pavilion then must be next to the northern river, which means we need to reach that edge with the placements of the Open Area (small tree), Hydrologist, White Stork, and second Open Area (small tree). Three out of the four need to be used to get one tile away from the river.
      • The second White Stork will need to be placed adjacent to the first one to get the second conservation marker needed to achieve the 10 point conservation.
      • The Tree House many turns later will need to be next to the two White Storks, so the first Open Area must be northeast of the entrance.
      • Ordering of tiles now forces the Hydrologist and the second Open Area to be placed in order.
    • The Sumatran Tiger only has a single legal placement along with its Open Area/trees as the 10th placement provided the Asia Pavilion is placed in the westernmost space along the northern river.
      • The Open Areas are forced since the trees can't shadow other trees.
    • It becomes tricky after this, and looking ahead to the building constraints ends up being the best way forward.
      • The Tree House needs to be next to the two White Storks.
      • The library needs to be next to the only two projects we have. These add up to use most of the row directly east of the entrance.
      • The Observation deck needs to be next to four distinct animal and/or continent icons. This will be satisfied by being next to the Sumatran Tiger and the Capybara.
    • The Ornithologist effect triggers on itself, causing a medium tree to be placed next. Due to shading constraints, it must be next to the northern river east of the big tree.
    • The Nile Crocodile has an Open Area requirement to the northeast, which completes the tree placements, and is the only tree left to provide shade to the Observation Deck.
    • The Greater Flamingo is forced by shading constraints.

    Eats Fish Shoots and Leaves

    The title of puzzle and the images on the tiles reference penguins, fish, pandas, and bamboos. The pandas and bamboos point us to Takenoko. Looking at the flavor text, Zen Garden and gardener are also about Takenoko. The ice floes go with the penguin and fish to suggest Hey, That's My Fish.

    Click to view detailed solution The tooltip says we need to retrace history, meaning that the board state we see is the final configuration, and we will need to figure out where each of the characters were on earlier turns. Looking at the rules and the iconography we can deduce:

    • Outside of game rules, the tiles cannot occupy the same space in this round, which means characters never occupy the same space (even on different turns) except for the very beginning.
    • All of the characters move in straight lines but can't move across gaps in the board.
    • Dotted lines seem to represent something missing.
      • There are missing tiles because of the penguin, causing gaps to form, and making it impossible to tell what color the original tile was.
      • There is missing bamboo because the panda ate them.
    • The gardener plants bamboo on the tile it lands on and all adjacent tiles of the same color, but a tile need to be irrigated for bamboo to grow.
      • The leftover bamboo should be accurate at the end of all the character movements.
    • The panda eats one piece of bamboo on the tile it lands on.
    • The penguin removes the tile that it leaves on its turn and must land on a tile with fish. This is how all the characters end up trapped, as clued by the flavor text.

    Following tooltip instructions, we look to the gardener tiles, and the long winded tooltips give us relatively clear instructions on where to place them. An odd phrase used a few times is rolling in, which clues that the Takenoko dice are in effect for some of the turns, helping to variously grow bamboo, and scare the panda away. The gardener also tells us that one of the terrible creatures (the penguin) is removing tiles as they go, just like how they behave in Hey, That's My Fish. If we place all the gardener tiles as instructed, we can't seem to arrive at the yellow island -- we ended up somewhere else. This last gardener tooltip tells us what to do -- take an initial look through my prior notes. Looking at the first letter of each of the Gardener tiles, we find out that they are COLORBLIND, specifically pink/green colorblind, as indicated by the double exclamation mark.

    Following the same tooltips with this information will solve this conundrum and place the gardener tiles correctly. If we also work through what bamboo the gardener would grow, this either acts as confirmation or a separate way to solve the gardener's pathing. The simplified instructions:

    1. Go all the way west.
    2. Avoid the panda (leaving either northeast or southeast), go as far as possible -- go southeast.
    3. Follow storm (west to east) slowly (one step).
    4. Head northwest (there's only one possible placement).
    5. Head to next greenpink (pink/green colorblindness) tile to the east.
    6. As far as possible in the same color, which is just two to the northeast, regardless of possible colorblindness.
    7. To a different color (must be yellow), then as far as possible, which is one step.
    8. A different color but slowly. There's only one possibility one step away, which also happens to be non-yellow.
    9. A leisurely stroll in the same direction. The gardener notices no change in color.
    10. Ambiguous, but the last tile placement forces this. It has no missing tiles adjacent to it at the time.
    11. A yellow island tile.

    For the panda, there are only four moves, two of which are described by the gardener. We know where the panda ends up (the transparent panda icon), which will help force the panda's placement on turn 3, where it is scared to any location by the storm rolling in.

    This leaves us the penguin's movements to consider. This is almost a separate little logic puzzle.

    • The penguin can't go across missing tiles.
    • The penguin always goes to the tile it can reach that has the most fish.
    • When two tiles have equal amounts of fish, we tie break by going to the one that is closer.
    • It needs to trap the panda by being next to the red island on turn 3, and trap the gardener on the yellow island on turn 11, before finally trapping itself by moving to the green island on turn 12.
    • At the gardener's turn 10, all of the surrounding tiles can't be missing.

    Given the provided tile ordering, this forces a unique solution.

    1. Forced by the gardener turn 10 requirement, going east 3 spaces.
    2. Forced by turn 3 requirement.
    3. Forced.
    4. Only one direction available, and both of the next tiles have two fish.
    5. The northernmost tile must be visited or it will be orphaned.
    6. Turn 7 is 3 fish, so we must change directions on the next turn. Looking ahead we can see that we must go to the further tile.
    7. Only one placement is allowed, since it can't be on the line of travel from the previous turn (most fish constraint).
    8. Only one placement.
    9. Only one placement.
    10. Only one placement.
    11. Only one placement.
    12. Must end on the transparent penguin on the green island.

    Looking for Bigfoot

    The flavor text "On your first foray searching the Pacific Northwest, be sure to take A look at the keystone wildlife -- each is worth A point or more." and the animals on the tiles are clues that we are looking for Cascadia as one of our games. The tooltips that reference waters, mountains, forests, deserts, and swamps; the background color of the level; and the mention of Bigfoot point to the other game: Cryptid.

    Click to view detailed solution

    Reading through the Cryptid rulebook, we can match up a few things;

    • The standing stones are the the octagons.
    • The ALL CAPS part of the tooltips are clues from cryptid. The animals represent cubes, which are tokens we must place on a tile we know the cryptid cannot be on -- explaining the contradicting placement of the preplaced cubes compared to the tooltip.
    • The background colors on the level match the terrain from Cryptid (desert, forest, mountain, swamp, water).
    • Once all the cubes are placed, there will be exactly one empty spot in the level -- where Bigfoot lives.

    On to Cascadia:

    • The animals are from Cascadia, and the lowercase tooltips translate their preferred habitats to Cryptid ones. The colors match up reasonably well (but prairies becoming deserts seems pretty tough for the salmon).
    • The flavor text with its miscapitalized "A"s and being told each wildlife is worth A point or more suggests we are looking for the A scoring cards.
      • Bears need to be strictly in pairs.
      • Hawks can't be next to each other.
      • Elks score no matter what.
      • Foxes can be anywhere adjacent to something.
      • Salmon must form groups of exactly three (triangles are ok) to score 8 points.

    With all the constraints roughly in place (the bear territory constraint depends on where the bear cubes are placed), we now have a logic puzzle on our hands. The bears are a good entry point -- needing to place 4 pairs of them not being within one space of deserts (yellow tiles), and only on waters, mountains, or forests leaves relatively little choice. Pair that with elks needing to be not within two spaces of bear territory, and only on mountains, forests, or deserts, and a pretty tight corridor forms for both species. Some joint constraints from both games start to emerge as well:

    • Forest tiles that are not within two tiles of a standing stone (which also happen to be adjacent to desert) must house elks if anything.
    • Forest tiles next to bears must house foxes if anything.

    Salmon groups start breaking up the board naturally, and hawks and foxes are the only possible buffers for those spaces.

    Extraction

    The extraction card is a list of 10 simple equations that ask us to calculate some numbers based on the solved levels.

    Sum of light points earned from trees if light came from the southeast: Sunshine East of Sanctuary. If we follow Photosynthesis rules, and place the sun coming from the southeast, out of the 9 trees (2 big, 3 medium, 4 small), there are two small trees that are in the shadow of a big tree.

    2x3 + 3x2 + 2x1 = 14.

    Number of curses avoided + number of neighbors adjacent to offices: From Jungle to Metropolis. 4 curses were avoided. The Office of Bureaucracy has 2 neighbors, and the Local EPA Office has 3 neighbors.

    4 + 2 + 3 = 9.

    Largest number of one type of wildlife in line of sight of the cryptid: Looking for Bigfoot. Line of sight rules come from Cascadia, and we know the cryptid is in the only tile not marked by a cube. Looking through the six edges, we see 7 salmon, which beats out the 4 foxes, 4 elk, 2 bears, or 1 hawk.

    Max(7, 4, 4, 2, 1) = 7.

    Number of steps the penguin took / the sum of bamboo segments on non-preplaced tiles next to the start: Eats Fish Shoots and Leaves. The penguin takes 24 total steps. The number of bamboo segments on placed tiles doesn't include the one the panda ate, or the two on the preplaced tile, which leaves 3.

    24 / 3 = 8.

    Sum of highest appeal points earned by each tile type (animal, project, building): Sunshine East of Sanctuary. The highest scoring animal is one of the White Storks, scoring 1 point for each Open Area -- 9.

    The highest scoring project is the Ornithologist, scoring 1 point for each bird icon -- 4.

    The highest scoring building is the Observation Deck, scoring 1 for each unique animal and continent icon adjacent to it -- 7.

    9 + 4 + 7 = 20.

    Number of jokers earned from surrounding fountains + number of cat tokens earned: The Palace and Its Cats. We surround 3 fountains, which nets a joker each. We attract 2 Almonds, 2 Rumis, and 1 Tecolote.

    3 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 8.

    Number of commercial tiles adjacent to schools - number of amulets obtained: From Jungle to Metropolis. The High School and Elementary school each have 1 blue tile next to it. 1 amulet was obtained.

    2 - 1 = 1.

    Total worth of the wildlife that has a habitat one tile east of the cryptid: Looking for Bigfoot. The wildlife one tile east of the cryptid is an Elk. The best we can do is 1 run of three, 2 runs of two, and 2 runs of one.

    9x1 + 5x2 + 2x2 = 23.

    Number of steps the Gardener took - number of steps the Panda took: Eats Fish Shoots and Leaves. The gardener took 20 steps. The panda took 9 steps.

    20 - 9 = 11.

    Sum of tile values for group that surrounds northern-most statue + number of rainbow tokens: The Palace and Its Cats. The northern-most statue is surrounded by 2 birds, 2 butterflies, and 2 flowers. This is 2x2 + 2x3 + 2x4 = 18. We only earn 1 rainbow token since we earn at most one button for each of the six colors.

    18 + 1 = 19.
