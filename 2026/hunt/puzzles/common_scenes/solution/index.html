    Solution to Common Scenes

    Answer: CARVING
    Written by: Herman Chau
    Edited by: Benji Nguyen
    Additional thanks to:
    • Guan Yang Ze for Solution writing

    Each of the pieces in this puzzle holds a drawing depicting one or more human figures in poses matching a particular painting. Some of the more famous paintings with very distinctive poses, such as Goya’s Saturn Devouring His Son or Botticelli’s The Birth of Venus, might help us come to this realization early on.

    We can start by identifying some of the paintings; as we proceed, we might notice that some of the drawings’ poses look remarkably similar to others in the pool. Combined with the title of the puzzle, this idea suggests that the puzzle is about scenes depicted by multiple artists: for example, Rubens has a (less well-known) painting of Saturn devouring his son.

    Now we can turn to the shell for extraction, which shows a grid of circles and some rectangular boundaries forming groups among the circles. Counting the objects in the diagram, we notice that there are 19 circles and 14 groups, corresponding nicely to our 19 unique artists and 14 “common scenes”. What’s more, we can count the number of circles in each group; summing up these group sizes gives 36, matching the number of paintings we have. We can conclude that each circle represents an artist and each group represents a scene, with a circle being in a group if and only if this puzzle includes a painting of that scene by that artist.

    All that remains is to identify each of the circles in this diagram, which we can do mostly uniquely by comparing the topology of the diagram with our known scene/artist groupings. In grid order, the circles correspond to artists as shown below. (Note that the positions of Gentileschi and Klimt are interchangeable, but this doesn’t affect the extraction.)
